    Long-Lasting PEX Pipe Installation Services in Glendale, AZ

    Whole-Home PEX Repiping in Glendale

    A whole-home repipe replaces every hot and cold supply line from the main shutoff to each fixture. In slab homes, we usually abandon the under-slab copper and run new PEX through the attic and down interior walls. This eliminates future slab leaks from the old lines and gives you a fresh system. Most homes are repiped in two to four days with water restored each evening when possible.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Glendale, AZ

    Gray polybutylene pipe was common in homes built from the late 1970s through the mid 1990s and is known for sudden failures. Chlorine in municipal water breaks down the plastic and its fittings over time. Many insurers now ask about polybutylene when writing a policy. Replacing it with PEX removes that risk and can make your home easier to insure and sell.

    Copper to PEX Conversions in Glendale

    Copper lines in Arizona often develop pinhole leaks from water chemistry, high velocity and sometimes soil contact under slabs. Once a home has had several pinhole leaks, more usually follow. Converting to PEX stops the cycle and reduces the risk of hidden water damage. We use approved transition fittings wherever new PEX connects to existing copper stubs or valves.

    PEX-A Expansion Fitting Systems in Glendale, AZ

    PEX-A, such as Uponor, uses an expansion tool to stretch the pipe and a ring over the fitting, which then shrinks back into a tight seal. The fittings have a larger inner diameter, so flow restriction is lower. PEX-A is also more flexible and can be repaired from kinks with a heat gun. It is our preferred choice for many full repipes.

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Glendale

    PEX-B is stiffer and more affordable, and it is joined with copper crimp rings or stainless steel cinch clamps over brass or poly fittings. It is a solid option for smaller projects and budget-conscious repipes. Brands like Viega, Zurn and SharkBite offer reliable PEX-B systems. We use a go/no-go gauge on every crimp to confirm a proper seal.

    Manifold and Home-Run PEX Layouts in Glendale, AZ

    A manifold system sends a dedicated line from a central hub to each fixture, similar to a breaker panel for water. Each line gets its own shutoff, which makes repairs easier and keeps pressure steady when multiple fixtures run. Home-run layouts use more pipe but fewer fittings in walls. We often combine manifold and trunk-and-branch designs to balance cost and performance.

    Remodel and Addition PEX Rough-In in Glendale

    Kitchen and bathroom remodels are a great time to replace old supply lines with PEX. We rough in new lines to the correct heights for vanities, showers, tubs and appliances, then pressure test before the walls are closed. Additions and casitas get properly sized lines tied into your existing system. We coordinate with your contractor to keep the project schedule on track.

    PEX Repipe Cost Comparison in Glendale, AZ

    PEX repipes typically cost less than copper repipes because the material is less expensive and installs faster. The total depends on the number of bathrooms, the home's size and how accessible the attic and walls are. Drywall patching and texture matching are often included or quoted separately. We give you a detailed estimate so you can compare PEX-A, PEX-B and copper options side by side.

    Frequently Asked Questions About PEX Pipe Installation in Glendale

    A whole-home PEX repipe typically costs $4,500 to $12,000 depending on home size, bathroom count and access. Smaller jobs like a single bathroom remodel rough-in often run $800 to $2,500.

    Whole-home repipes usually require a plumbing permit and inspection. We handle the permit process as part of the job.

    Properly installed PEX is expected to last many decades. Manufacturers commonly offer 25 year warranties when their pipe and fittings are used together.

    Multiple pinhole leaks, discolored water, low pressure throughout the house and gray polybutylene pipe are common signs. Recurring slab leaks are also a strong reason to repipe overhead.

    We make small, targeted openings to run new pipe and patch them when finished. Most homeowners are surprised by how little drywall work is needed.
