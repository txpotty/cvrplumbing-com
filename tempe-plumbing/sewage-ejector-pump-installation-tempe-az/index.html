    Heavy-Duty Sewage Ejector Pump Installation Services in Tempe, AZ

    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in Tempe

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so toilets, showers and sinks drain into a sealed basin instead of flowing by gravity. We excavate the floor, set a basin typically 30 to 50 gallons and connect each fixture drain. A solids-handling pump lifts waste up and into the main drain line. The basin lid is sealed and vented to keep odors out.

    Grinder Pump Installation in Tempe, AZ

    Grinder pumps use cutting blades to chop waste into slurry before pumping, which allows smaller discharge pipe and longer horizontal runs. They are used where waste must travel uphill a long distance or into a pressurized sewer. Grinder pumps cost more than standard ejectors but handle difficult layouts. We size them carefully because they draw more power and need a dedicated circuit.

    Upflush and Macerating Toilet Systems in Tempe

    Macerating systems like Saniflo let you add a toilet and sink without breaking the concrete floor. The unit sits behind the toilet and pumps waste through small-diameter pipe up to the drain line. They work well for small basement bathrooms, laundry rooms and casitas. We explain their limits, including what should never be flushed, so the system stays reliable.

    Laundry and Wet Bar Ejector Pumps in Tempe, AZ

    Below-grade laundry rooms and wet bars produce gray water that still needs to be lifted to the sewer line. A smaller ejector or laundry tray pump can handle washer discharge and sink drainage. We install a lint filter where needed to protect the pump. These systems are compact and can often be installed without major floor demolition.

    Ejector Pump Replacement in Tempe

    Ejector pumps typically last about 7 to 10 years, depending on use and what goes down the drains. Signs of trouble include constant running, frequent cycling, odors and slow fixture drainage. We remove the failed pump, inspect the basin, check valve and float, and install a new pump from brands like Zoeller, Liberty Pumps or Wayne. The system is tested with water before we leave.

    Duplex and Commercial Ejector Systems in Tempe, AZ

    Commercial buildings, multi-unit properties and homes with heavy below-grade use often need duplex systems with two pumps and an alternating control panel. If one pump fails, the other keeps working while an alarm alerts staff. We size the basin and pumps for peak flow and install controls to code. Maintenance schedules are provided to keep both pumps in good condition.

    Ejector Pump Alarms and Backup Options in Tempe

    A high water alarm is essential on any ejector system because a failure can quickly lead to sewage backup. We install audible alarms or Wi-Fi monitors that send alerts to your phone. Monsoon storms can cause power outages, so we can discuss generator connections for critical installations. These safeguards are inexpensive compared with sewage cleanup.

    Ejector Pump Installation Cost Comparison in Tempe, AZ

    Replacing a pump in an existing basin is the least expensive option. A new system with basin, floor excavation, venting and electrical costs more but enables a full below-grade bathroom. Macerating toilets often cost less upfront but have more limitations. We give you a detailed quote so you can compare a standard ejector, grinder pump and upflush system.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Tempe

    Replacing a pump in an existing basin typically costs $900 to $2,000. A new ejector system with basin, venting and floor work usually ranges from $2,500 to $6,000 or more.

    New ejector systems involve sealed basins, venting, electrical and permits, so we recommend a licensed plumber. Mistakes can lead to sewer gas leaks or backups.

    Avoid flushing wipes, paper towels, feminine products, grease and cat litter. These items clog pumps and cause early failure.

    New ejector installations typically require a plumbing permit and inspection. Straight pump replacements may not, and we confirm local requirements for each job.

    Pump replacements usually take two to four hours. New systems with floor excavation and venting typically take one to two days.

    A sump pump removes groundwater or seepage, while an ejector pump handles sewage and wastewater from fixtures. Ejector pumps are designed to pass solids and require a sealed, vented basin.

    Constant running, a sounding alarm, sewage odors, gurgling drains and slow drainage are common signs. Act quickly to avoid a backup.

    Most ejector pumps last 7 to 10 years with normal use. Flushing wipes or other non-flushable items can shorten that life considerably.
